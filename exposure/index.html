    ◐ Aperture

    A smaller f-number means a wider opening: more light and a thinner zone of sharpness. The lens and focus distance also shape that zone.

    f/2.8 → f/4 ≈ one stop less light

    ◷ Shutter speed

    A short exposure freezes motion. A long one records a trail. The subject’s pace stays the same while the camera records time differently.

    1/250 → 1/125 = one stop more light

    ✦ ISO

    Digital gain brightens the signal and its noise. It does not gather light. A wider aperture or slower shutter can collect more light and improve image quality.

    ISO 400 → 800 = one stop brighter
    What this simulator shows (and what it simplifies)

    This is an illustrated, fixed-camera simulation with a full-frame sensor, a choice of 24, 35, 50, 85 or 135 mm lenses and focus locked at 6 m. These are idealized lenses sharing an f/1.4–f/22 range, not specific lens products. A longer lens narrows the field of view and enlarges subjects and motion trails; it can crop the flowers or moving subject out of the frame. Changing the lens keeps the camera position and perspective fixed. At the same f-number, shutter and ISO, changing focal length does not change the exposure in this model. Depth of field uses a 0.03 mm circle of confusion. The flowers are at 3 m and the trees at 25 m. The path is simplified to the subject’s focus plane. Choose a slow walker (1.4 km/h), a leisurely cyclist (2.9 km/h), or a slowly driven car (8.6 km/h) on the park access road. These illustrative speeds remain constant when you change camera settings. Selecting another subject starts its pass at the center of the frame. Small-aperture diffraction, camera shake and lens aberrations are not modeled.

    Daylight is EV 15, overcast EV 12, golden hour EV 11, and dusk EV 8 at ISO 100. The meter compares rendered brightness with a nominal middle tone; zero is a starting point, not a creative rule. Very bright exposures clip highlights.

    Noise is illustrative, not a prediction of any particular camera. Aperture, shutter time and scene light determine how much light reaches the sensor. ISO changes digital gain, amplifying the signal and its noise. Less collected light gives a poorer signal-to-noise ratio, even when raising ISO restores brightness.

    Capture keeps a simulated exposure of the preceding shutter interval, without stopping the scene. Pausing holds the subject’s position for comparison; shutter blur still represents their motion. The contact sheet keeps the latest 12 photos in this tab until removed or reloaded; download the PNG to keep a copy with its settings. Capture two photos to open the A/B wipe, then use Set A and Set B to choose what to compare. The comparison slider supports touch and arrow keys. The camera model is digital; film may come later.
